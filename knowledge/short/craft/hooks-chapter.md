# Chapter-level hooks: Chapter beginning/end hook selection and writing


> This document is adapted from the short flow package (oh-story series + actual
> measurement adjustment) received as a gift, and is adapted to the caliber of this repo: the reference is the post-deployment base path, and external system
> references have been eliminated. Confirmation by the original author is required
> before disclosure or commercial use.


> Load as you write the beginning or end of each chapter. First look at the
> decision-making route, choose the right hook type, and then apply the template.

---

## Decision Routing

### End-of-Chapter Hook: Choose the type based on the mood of this chapter

| Mood/stage of this chapter | Recommended hook | Why |
|-------------|---------|-------|
| Suspense/Unknown | Sudden revelation/Mysterious object/Blank space | Arouse curiosity |
| Urgency/Crisis | Urgent Crisis / Countdown / Unfinished Action | Create Anxiety |
| Reversal/Truth | Identity Reversal / Hidden Meaning / Echo | Subverting Perception |
| Emotions/Relationships | Dilemmas / Commitment Threats / Emotional Turns | Pulling Emotions |
| System-specific | System activation / selection type | Looking forward to golden finger |
| Atmosphere/Daily | Imagery hooks / Bizarre disappearance / Information gap | Undercurrent |

### Chapter opening hook: choose the type according to the opening strategy

| Opening Strategy | Recommended Hooks | Why |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Direct impact | Unfinished action start / Countdown start | Instant sense of urgency |
| Create curiosity | Suspense dialogue / pre-flash fragments / mysterious monologue | Information gap drive |
| Contrast Impact | Contrast Scene/Image Prediction | Contrast Attraction |

### Chapter Stages and Hook Strength

| Story Stages | Recommended Types | Strength |
|----------|---------|------|
| Chapter 1 | System activation / truncation / crisis escalation | Must be strong |
| Chapter 2-3 | Choice / Information Gap / Emotional Turn | Strong |
| Mid-term daily life | Conversation suspense / Information gap / Identity is about to be revealed | Medium |
| Before the climax | Escalation of crisis / Suspense prediction | Strong |
| Finale | Time jump / emotional turning point | Ending |

## Chapter tail hook type 13

| # | Name | Formula | Example |
|---|------|------|------|
| 1 | Sudden revelation | Throw out information that changes the situation | "The date on the letter is the seventh day after his death." |
| 2 | Urgent crisis | An urgent threat that must be responded to in the next chapter | "The cracks are widening, and there are still three spiritual stones left." |
| 3 | Unfinished action | Action interrupted by new variable | "As soon as he reached out, his wrist was suddenly grabbed. 'Don't move.' A voice came from behind." |
| 4 | Identity reversal | The identity truth deviates from readers' expectations | "Her name is Lin Xiaoyue. But the file says: Lin Xiaoyue, deceased." |
| 5 | Dilemma | Forced to choose one of two bad options | "Sign this document and the company is saved, but he has to get in." |
| 6 | Mysterious Item | An important but unknown object | "Inside the package was a key, with a note attached: 'You owe me.'" |
| 7 | Countdown | Not enough time | "The doctor said there are still three months. That was two months ago." |
| 8 | Promise/Threat | Someone announces an intention to act | "Before twelve o'clock tonight, I will tell everyone what you did." |
| 9 | Bizarre disappearance | Impossible disappearance | "The handcuffs are still there, but the person is gone. There is only one door in the whole room, and it has never been opened." |
| 10 | Hidden meaning | Normal on the surface, but hidden information | "He said: 'You and your sister are really alike.' But she is an only child." |
| 11 | Imagery hook | Changes in recurring images | "The pot of jasmine that had been dry for a month on the windowsill suddenly sprouted buds." |
| 12 | Echo hook | Ending sentence echoes the beginning, key details change | Beginning: "She said she would never forgive him." / Ending: "She said she would never forgive him. But her hand tightened." |
| 13 | Blank hook | Only show reactions, not what happened | "He read the letter. His face changed. He said nothing, folded the letter and put it in his pocket." |

## Chapter Head Hook 7 Styles

| # | Name | Formula | Example |
|---|------|------|------|
| 1 | Suspense dialogue start | Start directly with meaningful dialogue | "Are you sure you want to do this? / Sure. / Then don't regret it." |
| 2 | Pre-flash fragments | Give the result fragments first, and then return to the normal narrative | "Later he learned that the phone call changed everything. But at the moment he didn't know anything." |
| 3 | Countdown start | Establish a sense of urgency at the beginning | "There are still 72 hours until the contract expires." |
| 4 | Mysterious monologue | First-person weird inner monologue | "I have been thinking, if I had not looked back that day, everything would have been different." |
| 5 | Contrast scenes | Two completely different scenes juxtaposed | "On one side is the wedding scene, with champagne towers piled high. On the other side, the lights in the hospital corridor are flashing." |
| 6 | Start of unfinished action | The action was cut off in progress | "He had just inserted the key into the keyhole, and there was a soft sound from the door." |
| 7 | Imagery foreshadowing | Use environmental imagery to hint at what is about to happen | "The sky was burning red, as if something was there. She looked up and continued walking." |

## End of Chapter Practical Template

### 1. System activation type
```
Just when {the protagonist} is in his lowest state.
{a sensory description}.
Ding!
[{system name}, officially started. 】
```
> Just when he was desperate. A cold mechanical voice suddenly sounded in his mind.
> bite! [Young and frivolous system is officially launched. 】

### 2. Suspense and prediction type
```
{Protagonist} looks {calmly/indifferently} at {the other party's arrogant behavior}.
{A short sentence: go ahead/take your time/take your time}.
{Predict the upcoming reversal, without revealing details}.
```
> I looked coldly at the man who was laughing so hard. Just laugh. Laugh more. After
> today, you will never laugh again.

### 3. Truncated type
```
{A small gesture}.
{Violent reaction}.
{Shocked/Doubtful. End of chapter, no answers}.
```
> Just one bite! Her pupils suddenly shrank into pinpoint shapes. boom! A terrifying
> indescribable heat instantly exploded in her belly! There’s something wrong with
> this porridge! What exactly is inside?

### 4. Selective type
```
[Choice 1: {Attractive option}] Reward: {Extremely attractive} Price: {Inplicit risk}
[Choice 2: {Another option}] Reward: {Another temptation} Price: {Another risk}
```
The chapter ends without giving the selection result.

### 5. Crisis escalation type
```
{Sudden sound/movement}!
{New threat emerges}.
{Protagonist’s fear/urgency reaction}.
```
> "Come out, everyone! Hurry up!" Bang!!! A muddy military boot roughly kicked open
> the cage door.

### 6. Identity is about to be revealed
```
{A bystander} suddenly {raises questions/finds clues}.
"{Words that hint at the protagonist's true identity}"
{Others didn’t take it seriously, and the protagonist was secretly nervous}.
```

### 7. Emotional transition type
```
At this moment, {character}'s {expression/attitude} suddenly {changed}.
{An unexpected gesture of weakness/request}.
"{If you hit the protagonist's weak spot}"
```

### 8. Information difference type
```
{Protagonist’s self-doubt/sentiment}.
{an image/metaphor}.
{hints of imminent change, but doesn't say}.
```
> Flowers will bloom again, and people will never be young again. Why did you lead
> your life like this?

### 9. Dialogue suspense type
```
"{First half of the sentence making the request}"
"{The other party responds}"
"{The unexpected second half of the sentence is not what the reader expected}"
```
> "I have a request." Su Mu said slowly. "You said." "Before divorce, I have to prove
> myself once."

### 10. Time jump type
```
{Exact/fuzzy time stamp}.
{Unexpected results}.
{A sentence that hints at the process}.
```
> Three minutes and three seconds later. Jiang Yiyao walked out of the room calmly and
> straightened out her slightly messy clothes.

## Three sketch end-of-chapter hooks

### Harvest preview type (dungeon/end of mission)
```
{The current operation is nearly complete}.
{hinting at upcoming rewards}.
{A sentence suggesting that the reward is greater than expected}.
```

### Villain approaching type (end of foreshadowing section)
```
{Current status of the protagonist}.
{In the dark/far away, a certain action of the villain}.
{implies that this action will affect the protagonist}.
```

### Action preview type (before showing off/face-slapping)
```
{The protagonist makes a decision}.
{a preparatory action}.
{hint of upcoming action}. Do not write results.
```

## Satisfying payoff and interesting design rules

### Satisfying Payoff Core Formula

satisfying payoff = conflict point between two logics:
- **Popular logic**: The opponent is invincible → Fear/Despair
- **Protagonist Logic**: It just feels itchy/understatement
- The bigger the gap → the more satisfying the payoff is.

### Three elements of satisfying payoff

| Role | Action |
|------|------|
| The melon-eating crowd | Use arguments to inflate your opponent’s strength → Shocked reaction completes face-slapping sublimation |
| Opponent | Arrogant enough to make readers hate → It’s cool to be face-slapping |
| Protagonist | Low-key when setting up → Crisp when releasing |

Highlights = raising readers’ expectations; satisfying payoff = satisfying expectations.
One-to-one correspondence: The amount of foreshadowing in the front will determine the
satisfying payoff in the back.

### Pretentious face-slapping rhythm

| Stage | Public period (5 chapters cycle) | VIP period |
|------|-------------------|--------|
| Character | Chapter 1 | Chapter 1-2 |
| Foreshadowing | Chapter 2 | Chapter 5 |
| face-slapping | Chapter 1 | Chapter 1-2 |
| Aftermath | Chapter 1 | Chapter 1 |

**Key**:
- Pre-prepared Little Invincible: The props/characters/strength must be prepared before
  face-slapping. If not, readers will be frightened and ridicule will turn into abuse.
- Foreshadowing is more important than face-slapping: once the foreshadowing is in
  place, the protagonist can act cool even while standing without speaking.
- The level of face-slapping objects determines the level of pleasure: it is not
  pleasure to say that a passerby is shocked, but it is pleasure to be shocked when a
  person with weight is shocked.
- An end-of-chapter hook of about 100 words can significantly increase the reading rate

## Quality check

Compare after writing the beginning/end of the chapter:

- [ ] The first 100 words of the chapter have a hook (not the beginning of
      scenery/weather)
- [ ] There is something at the end of the chapter that makes the reader want to turn
      the next page
- [ ] The hook strength matches the chapter stage (the hook does not need to be strong
      in daily chapters)
- [ ] Don’t use the same hook for two consecutive chapters
- [ ] The hook is about 100 words long, just hit the mark and don’t delay
