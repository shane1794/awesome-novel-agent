## 1. AI writing fingerprint

The text generated by AI has stable feature patterns at the statistical level. The
following is the basis for fingerprint recognition.

### 1.1 High-frequency word fingerprint

The AI prefers certain high-frequency words that are sparsely distributed in human
writing:

| Category | Typical words |
|------|--------|
| Modal adverbs | suddenly, as if, can't help, still, gradually |
| Weakening of judgment | It seems, maybe, probably, some, some kind |
| Written link | However, at the same time, it can be seen that, in summary |
| Universal verb | reveal, sink into, surge, emerge, flash past |
| Psychological guidance | Feel, feel, realize, understand |
| Written fill | In order to achieve this goal, due to the fact that..., at this point in time |

### 1.2 Summary at the end of chapter

AI has a strong tendency to summarize and sublimate at the end of chapters:

- "He didn't know he was about to face..."
- "From this moment on, everything will be different"
- "This night is destined to..."
- "Perhaps, this is the meaning of growth"

### 1.3 Superimposed description

AI tends to use multiple layers of modifications to create a "literary feel":

- Multi-layered attributive: "A complex and unspeakable emotion flashed in his eyes that
  even he himself could not explain clearly."
- Emotion + action + environment three-piece set: "He took a deep breath and looked at
  the distant skyline, with mixed feelings in his heart."
- Adverb stacking: "He seemed to vaguely feel..."

### 1.4 Uniform distribution

AI has a stable and even distribution tendency at the micro level:

- **Even sentence length**: The fluctuation range of long and short sentences is small,
  and very short sentences of 2-3 words rarely appear.
- **Even paragraphs**: The distribution of paragraph lengths is concentrated, lacking
  very short paragraphs (1-2 sentences) and very long paragraphs (10+ sentences)
- **Even word frequency**: The frequency distribution of each word is flat and there is
  a lack of reasonable repetition of core words.
- **Even punctuation**: The proportion of periods and commas is stable, and there is a
  lack of unconventional punctuation such as dashes and pauses.
- **Synonym cycle**: driven by "don't repeat", AI uses different words to refer to the
  same thing in each paragraph ("protagonist/main character/central character"
  rotation); real people have personal high-frequency words, and core nouns and terms
  repeatedly use the same word.
- **Even subject completion**: AI completes the subject in almost every sentence;
  real-life Chinese omits a large number of subjects, especially in spoken narratives

## 2. Core Rules

### Rule 1: Paragraph Density

| Principles | Description |
|------|------|
| Only tell one thing in a single paragraph | Action, environment, dialogue, and emotion should not be crammed into the same paragraph |
| Decreasing information density | Key information first, supplementary information last |
| Short paragraphs are preferred | Can be said in 2 sentences but not in 5 sentences |

Ideal paragraph length distribution (in number of sentences, qualitative guideline, not
a hard indicator):

| Paragraph type | Recommended proportions | Function |
|---------|---------|------|
| Single sentence paragraphs | Recommended 10%-30% | Rhythm changes, emphasis (do not use single sentence paragraphs throughout the entire chapter) |
| 2-5 sentences | Main narrative | Regular paragraphs, moderate information density |
| 6+ segments | Use on demand | Complex scenarios allowed, please be careful not to overly uniform information density |

### Rule 2: Action + Dialogue + Emotion

Priority of emotional description:

1. **Action** (Best): "He slapped the cup on the table"
2. **Dialogue** (second best): "What do you mean?"
3. **BODY REACTION** (AGAIN): "His hands are shaking"
4. **Direct statements** (worst, only 1-2 short direct statements are allowed at key
   emotional nodes): "He is very angry"

### Rule 3: Combine long and short sentences

- Alternate long and short sentences naturally, avoid long sentences or short sentences
  throughout the article
- Short sentences can be used to highlight key information, and long sentences can be
  used to describe the atmosphere.
- It is recommended to separate short sentences between long sentences (>40 words) to
  adjust the rhythm.

### Rule 4: Be colloquial

- Conversations should be short, broken up, and have white space
- The narrative language favors a spoken rhythm rather than a written composition.
- Daily spoken words such as "then", "anyway" and "actually" are allowed, but low
  frequency

## 3. Show Don’t Tell principle

### 3.1 Core formula

```
Tell: emotional word/judgment sentence → the reader is informed
Show: body reaction/action/environment/dialogue → readers’ own feelings
```

### 3.2 Comparison table

| Tell (forbidden) | Show (recommended) |
|-------------|-------------|
| He was very nervous | He tapped his fingers on the edge of the table three times, and three more times |
| She was very angry | She put her phone screen down on the table |
| This is a dilapidated town | Red bricks are exposed where the walls are peeling off, and sheets are drying in the alleys |
| He felt uneasy | He took two steps and looked back |
| She was very happy | Her toes tapped the ground twice |
| The atmosphere was awkward | He lowered his head to drink water, and after drinking, he found that she was still looking at him |
| He was very confused | He lit up the cigarette, took a puff and put it out |

### 3.3 Five senses check

For each scene description, ask yourself:

- [ ] Vision - What did he see? (Not "beautiful", but "cracks in the wall")
- [ ] Hearing - What does he hear? (Not "quiet", but "only the clock is running")
- [ ] Touch - what does he feel? (It's not "cold", it's "the door handle is iced")
- [ ] Smell - What does he smell? (It's not "fragrance", it's "the soup was so bitter
      that he wrinkled his whole face")
- [ ] Kinesthetic - What is his body doing? (It's not "standing", it's "he changed his
      feet to support his center of gravity")

## 4. AI writing mode detection

### Mode 1: Summary beginning/end

```
Signal: Summary judgment appears at the beginning/end of the paragraph
Characteristics: "This is it" "This means" "He got it" "In general"
Repair: Delete summary and stop on specific scenes/actions
```

### Mode 2: Inner Analysis Report

```
Signal: A long and organized psychological description (>3 consecutive psychological sentences)
Characteristics: "He knew deep down..." "On the one hand...and on the other hand..."
Repair: broken into 1-2 sentences, and the rest externalized with actions
```

### Mode 3: Perfect Logic Chain

```
Signal: Each sentence is a direct cause and effect of the previous sentence.
Features: No idle strokes, no distractions, and no irrelevant details
Repair: insert an irrelevant observation between two sentences of cause and effect
```

### Mode 4: Uniform information density

```
Signal: Each paragraph promotes the plot, there is no change in density
Features: The information concentration is consistent from beginning to end, and there is no breathing period.
Repair: Select a paragraph and insert life-like details (distracted/looking out the window/thinking of irrelevant things)
```

### Pattern 5: Template Emotional Response

```
Signal: Emotional response is "the most reasonable option"
Characteristics: Cry when you should cry, get angry when you should be angry, be nervous when you should be nervous, the whole process is impeccable
Repair: Change one part to a misplaced reaction - laugh when you should cry, be dazed when you should be angry, and go off topic when you should be nervous
```

### Mode 6: Smooth across the board

```
Signal: All paragraph transitions are smooth and natural
Features: No sudden jumps, smooth transition at the beginning of each paragraph
Fix: If all paragraph transitions throughout the chapter are smooth and unchanged, consider deleting 1-2 transitional sentences to create a rhythm change.
Note: Don’t cut hard for the sake of hard cutting,natural transitions are necessary.
```

### Mode 7: Regular punctuation

```
Signal: Stable ratio of periods to commas, lack of unconventional punctuation
Features: Almost no use of dashes, pauses, and ellipses; little change in sentence width
Repair: Add 1-2 fragments of sentences using pauses/ellipses to create a sense of irregular punctuation.
```

### Mode 8: Symmetric fill

```
Signal: The sentence structure is symmetrical and neat, with balanced front and rear components.
Features: "Both X and Y" "Not only X but also Y" "From A to B"
Repair: Break the symmetry, keep only the strong side, delete the weak side directly or change it into an independent short sentence
      Not "both nervous and excited" → "He was so nervous that his palms were sweating. He was also a little excited."
      Not "from morning to dusk" → "It's light. It's dark again."
```

### Mode 9: Mechanical enumeration

```
Signal: Explicit sequential or parallel structure that reads like an outline
Features: "First...Second...Finally" "First...Second...Third" "On the one hand...On the other hand..."
Repair: Break it into a natural narrative, use time/causation/feeling succession instead of serial words
```

### Mode 10: Rhetorical questions and hypothesis guidance

```
Signal: Ask a rhetorical question/hypothesis before answering, creating unnecessary suspense
Features: "What if I told you..." "Have you ever thought about..." "Imagine..."
Repair: Delete the introductory sentence and state it directly. The reader does not need to be asked first and then told.
```

### Mode 11: Positive ending

```
Signal: Chicken soup/inspirational/hopeful summary appears at the end of the chapter
Features: "The future is promising", "Let's wait and see", "The future is long" (including variations)
Fix: Delete, stop on specific screen/action. The emotions are left to the reader to digest.
```

### Mode 12: Passive Reference

```
Signal: Mention research/data/evidence without citing sources
Features: "Research shows" "According to statistics" "Survey shows"
Repair: Delete "research shows" or add specific sources. Most of the novel can be deleted directly.
```

### Pattern 13: Coupling verb avoidance

```
Signals: Replace simple judgments of "yes/have" with complex structures
Features: "As... is..." "Has... provides..." - AI avoids linking verbs to create a sense of writing
Repair: Revert to direct judgment ("As an emergency doctor, he knows very well" → "He has been an emergency doctor, so he knows very well")
```

### Pattern 14: Over-qualified stacking

```
Signal: Using "maybe", "maybe" and "maybe" together in a sentence stacks up uncertainty
Characteristics: The judgment itself is vague, and multiple vague words are used to add insurance.
Repair: Keep one (or delete them all) and make judgments based on specific observations.
```

## 5. Systematic three-pass method to remove AI

### Pass 1: De-generalization (~80% of the AI flavor is concentrated here)

**Goal**: Replace abstract/clichéd expressions with concrete details.

Steps:
1. Full-text scanning of banned words (★★★★★ The most poisonous sentence pattern →
   first-level banned words)
2. Replace modal words (as if/a trace of/cannot help → delete or outline)
3. Replace emotion words (angry/sad → physical reaction)
4. Replace template description (a flash in the eye → action)
5. Replace the sentence pattern (not A but B → write B directly)

Verification: The hit rate of banned words dropped by more than 80%.

### Pass 2: Go to writing

**Objectives**: Written accent/translated accent → spoken language and movement.

Steps:
1. Scan for omniscient narration (He doesn’t know... → Delete)
2. Scan psychological analysis (deep inside his heart → externalized actions)
3. Go Inner Debate (One voice says... → Withhold conclusion)
4. Simplified dialogue tags (he said angrily → action takeover)
5. Remove adverb modification (he slowly → delete)

Verification: Dialog tag density <10%, no omniscient narration.

### Pass 3: Return to the sense of nature

**Goal**: Give the text traces of someone writing it,natural pauses and rhythm changes.

Steps:
1. Check if the rhythm is too even - if so, create natural fluctuations (alternating
   lengths and lengths, changes in information density)
2. Fill in the spare pen as appropriate (only when the information density is uniform
   throughout the article: fill in the life-like details at the positions belonging to
   the current scene and characters, and do not serve the main line)
3. Correct the misalignment according to the situation (only when the emotional response
   is "the most reasonable" throughout: change a position where the character's
   personality or the pressure of the scene can be supported)
4. Edit the ending (sublimation/summary/preview → picture/action)

Verification: Paragraph lengths vary naturally; idle strokes and misalignments (if any)
come from the current scene and characters and are not inserted according to quotas.
Avoid deliberate fragmentation of sentences.

### Scene level rewriting intensity (paragraph type × rewriting extent)

Different paragraph types have different rewriting intensity - manslaughter protection
(see boundary-cases.md) determines "where not to touch", and this table determines "how
deep to touch":

| Paragraph types | Recognition signals | Overwriting intensity | Protection terms |
|---------|---------|---------|--------|
| Narrative paragraph | Action description, scene advancement | Rewrite (can be rewritten in large sections) | Plot direction, key actions |
| Dialogue segments | Quotes, dialogue tags, Q&A | Light modification (only tags and rhythm are changed) | Original text of lines, numbers, attribution |
| Inner section | Judgment, association, recall | Intermediate modification (change sentence structure, preserve content) | The direction of judgment is not reversed |
| Memories | Past tense, specific dates, past events | Light modification (to maintain the skeleton of facts) | Timeline, names, event details |
| Quote external text field | Letter, screen original text, message original text | Do not change | Keep verbatim |

## 6. Rewrite the example library

### 6.1 Externalization of emotions

**Angry:**

❌ "An uncontrollable anger surged in his heart"

✅ "He slammed the cup on the table. Water splashed out and dripped down the edge of the
table. He didn't wipe it."

**Nervous:**

❌ "He was so nervous that his palms were sweating"

✅ "He rubbed his hand on his trouser leg. Then he rubbed it again. The edge of the table
was shiny from his touch."

**Sad:**

❌ "She felt an unspeakable sadness"

✅ "She folded her coat three times and put it back in the back of the closet. The zipper
was not pulled all the way. She unzipped it again and zipped it up again."

**surprise:**

❌ "He was so shocked that he couldn't speak"

✅ "He opened his mouth. No sound came out. He opened it again, but still no sound came
out."

### 6.2 Scene description

**Environment:**

❌ "As the sun sets, the clouds on the horizon are dyed golden red, it is breathtakingly
beautiful"

✅ "At a quarter past six, the sun has not yet set. The shadow is very long, and his
shadow is two heads taller than himself. The clouds are not red, but gray, like unclean
rags."

**Weather:**

❌ "It's raining so hard, it's like there's a hole in the sky"

✅ "The rain was hitting the tin shed so loudly that I couldn't hear anyone talking. His
trousers were soaked and there was water in his shoes."

**Building:**

❌ "The castle stands tall and towering, its spire reaches into the sky"

✅ "There were cracks in the castle wall. He counted them as he passed by, and there were
seven. The longest one could fit two fingers through."

### 6.3 Fighting

❌ "He dodged the opponent's attack and then hit his opponent in the chest with a
roundhouse kick."

✅ "The opponent came with a punch. He tilted his head, slowed down for a moment, and the
wind of the punch brushed his ears, which made him buzz. He didn't roundhouse kick - he
was not that neat - he bumped forward and hit his forehead on the bridge of the
opponent's nose."

### 6.4 Ending

**Error demonstration:**

❌ "He looked at the distant skyline, filled with expectations for the future. What he
didn't know was that greater challenges were waiting for him ahead. This night was
destined to be extraordinary."

✅ "He put out the cigarette. The sole of his shoe crushed him. He turned around and
walked back."

**Error demonstration:**

❌ "From this moment on, he finally understood that true courage is not to be fearless,
but to move forward despite fear"

✅ "He stood up. There were two blades of grass on his knees. He patted them but didn't
wipe them off. Forget it."

### 6.5 Rhythm comparison

**AI Uniform Rhythm:**

"He walked into the room and looked around. The room was quiet, except for the clock. He
opened the curtains, the sun shone in, and dust floated in the light beam. He sat down
on the edge of the bed, and the mattress bounced."

**Real person broken rhythm:**

"He opened the door. The clock was ticking. There was no other sound. The moment the
curtains opened, the ashes stirred in the beam of light and sank again. He sat down on
the bed and bounced twice."

### 6.6 Body response replacement

**Pupillary shrinks:**

❌ "His pupils shrank slightly"

✅ "He looked at it for three seconds. He lowered his head and turned a page of the
document. He turned it back and read it again."

**gasp:**

❌ "He took a breath"

✅ "He leaned back. The back of the chair hurt his back. He sat forward again."

**The body stiffened:**

❌ "His body stiffened"

✅ "His feet stayed in place. Someone walked around him and he didn't notice."

**Heart surges:**

❌ "A warm feeling surged in his heart"

✅ "He held the cup of tea in his hand. He didn't drink it. He didn't put it down even
when the tea got cold."

**Exposed/Face exposed:**

❌ "A smile appeared on his face"

✅ "He smiled. Halfway through, he found no one answered, so he took it back."

## 7. Quality Check List

### Self-check after modification

- [ ] None ★★★★★ The most poisonous sentence pattern
- [ ] No level one forbidden words
- [ ] Second-level banned words do not exceed the threshold
- [ ] Each paragraph only tells one thing
- [ ] Paragraph lengths vary naturally to avoid uniformity across the board
- [ ] Idle writing (if any) has a source - distraction/irrelevant details come from the
      current scene and characters, not inserted according to quota
- [ ] The emotional contrast (if any) comes from the character's personality or the
      pressure of the scene, not a formulaic contrast
- [ ] Dialogue tags are not dense (no 3 consecutive sentences with the same tag)
- [ ] The ending stops at the picture/action, without sublimation, summary or preview.
- [ ] There is no metaphor of "as if/like/like"
- [ ] The use of dashes complies with common-rules.md usage judgment (not counted)
- [ ] Paragraphs vary in length and there are no consecutive paragraphs of the same
      length
- [ ] Emotions rely on actions/conversations/body reactions, not direct psychological
      statements
- [ ] The plot is consistent with the original text, no new/deleted plots
